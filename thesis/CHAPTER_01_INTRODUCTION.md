# CHAPTER 1
# INTRODUCTION

Polymer/clay nanocomposites are among the most thoroughly studied filler-reinforced polymer systems, with reports of order-of-magnitude stiffness gains at very low filler loadings of montmorillonite (MMT) (Sinha Ray & Okamoto, 2003; Hussain et al., 2006; Ray, 2014). Two decades of literature, however, reveal a behaviour that is far less uniform than the headline numbers suggest: the same nominal filler can stiffen a soft elastomer by a factor of more than fifty while leaving a high-modulus epoxy almost unchanged, and the same polymer matrix can become tougher in one experiment and more brittle in another, depending on the modification of the clay and the resulting dispersion morphology (Manias et al., 2001; Azeez et al., 2013). The magnitude — and even the sign — of the mechanical response is therefore not an intrinsic property of the polymer/clay pair, but a function of the *mechanical regime* in which the unreinforced matrix already operates. Glassy thermosets, brittle semi-crystalline thermoplastics, moderately ductile commodity plastics, and rubbery elastomers each constitute a qualitatively distinct response regime — and this thesis treats those four regime levels not as a vague intuition but as an explicit, four-level partition of the matrix strain-to-failure axis. The same regime dependence holds for matrix stiffness and matrix strength. It is well known to practitioners but is rarely treated as a first-class variable in data-driven analyses of the polymer/clay literature, which tend to fit a single global model across all matrices and report aggregate statistics that mask the underlying regime structure.

This thesis argues that *polymer/clay nanocomposite performance is not governed only by filler loading or modification status, but by a regime-dependent network topology in which matrix stiffness, dispersion state, modification, and mechanical-property trade-offs form recurring structure-property motifs*. The *regime* — the mechanical state of the polymer/clay system, encompassing the unreinforced matrix's stiffness, strength, and ductility regimes together with the magnitude and direction of its response to clay reinforcement — is the organizing variable that has been missing from data-driven studies of polymer/clay nanocomposites, and making it explicit is what enables principled knowledge extraction from the existing experimental corpus.

Concretely, a regime-aware representation is built in two complementary ways. First, the mechanical-property space is partitioned into **twenty-eight physically meaningful regimes**, organized as **four discrete levels on each of seven property axes** ($4 \times 7 = 28$). The seven axes group into three families:

- **Matrix-state axes (three axes):** matrix elastic modulus (soft / medium / high / rigid), matrix strength (weak / medium-low / medium / strong, corresponding to the UBMK 2026 strength clusters C1–C4), and matrix strain-to-failure (glassy / brittle / moderate / ductile).
- **Response axes (three axes):** modulus improvement, strength improvement, and strain-to-failure change — each partitioned into four levels covering degradation, modest gain, strong gain, and extreme gain, so that both the magnitude *and* the sign of the response are preserved.
- **Loading axis (one axis):** clay weight fraction (sub-1 wt% / 1–3 wt% / 3–7 wt% / above 7 wt%), the standard processing-window quartiles for MMT-polymer systems.

Each of the twenty-eight regime bins is itself promoted to a first-class entity of the knowledge graph, rather than being treated as a latent stratum of a global statistical model; the four-level partitioning is applied uniformly to every axis so that the regime structure is dimensionally consistent across the matrix, response, and loading families.

Second, the categorical degrees of freedom of the dataset (polymer matrix, clay modification, dispersion morphology, polymer family, test method, data source, source article) are organized into an ontological knowledge graph, in the spirit of the generative knowledge-graph framework recently introduced by Buehler (2024) for unstructured materials-science text. The result is a single graph in which every experiment is co-located with the categorical concepts it instantiates *and* with the mechanical regimes it occupies, and in which semantic similarity between concepts can be measured directly via transformer-based node embeddings. This regime-aware knowledge graph is the data-driven knowledge-extraction substrate that the title of this thesis refers to.

## 1.1 Problem Definition

The central problem addressed in this thesis is that current data-driven analyses of polymer/clay nanocomposites are *regime-blind*, and that this blindness systematically distorts the structure-property relationships that such analyses are intended to surface.

Existing data-driven studies of the field fall into two broad camps. The first camp builds regression, micromechanical, or machine-learning models that predict an aggregate quantity such as the elastic modulus of the composite from a feature vector of matrix properties, filler content, and aspect ratio (Weon, 2009; Zaïri et al., 2011; Wang et al., 2013; Heydari-Meybodi et al., 2015; Zare & Rhee, 2017; Zazoum et al., 2020; Akbarzadeh et al., 2024). These models are accurate in aggregate but treat the dataset as a flat feature space and offer no mechanism for asking *which polymers behave like which other polymers, and under what conditions*. The second camp consists of single-system case studies that report mechanical behaviour for one polymer family at a time and explain it through chemistry-specific mechanisms; these studies are rich in physical insight but are difficult to aggregate into transferable design rules (Plummer et al., 2005; Voicu et al., 2024). In neither camp is the *regime* in which a matrix sits made explicit as a variable; regime is implicit in the choice of polymer and therefore conflated with chemistry.

The regime-blindness problem has three concrete consequences for the analysis of polymer/clay data. First, summary statistics computed across the full dataset are dominated by extreme regimes: the high percentage-improvement values reported for very soft matrices (e.g., poly(methyl acrylate) with sub-MPa neat strength) inflate the apparent benefit of clay reinforcement when no separation by regime is performed. Second, the standard $\log_{10}$ transformation routinely applied to property-improvement columns silently discards every experiment in which an improvement is negative, which is the dominant case for elongation-to-failure: in the dataset analysed here, $\log_{10}$ leaves only 91 of 440 elongation-improvement rows usable, biasing the entire transformed view toward stiffness/strength enhancement and away from the ductility losses that any honest discussion of clay reinforcement must address. Third, the relational structure of the data — the fact that a given polymer is co-investigated with specific clay modifications, dispersion morphologies, and characterization methods, and that these co-occurrence patterns themselves carry information — is not preserved by flat-feature representations.

A representation that addresses all three consequences must simultaneously (i) encode mechanical regimes explicitly so that within-regime analysis is possible, (ii) admit negative improvement values so that ductility losses and other degradations are first-class data, and (iii) preserve the relational structure that connects polymers, modifications, dispersions, and regimes through their shared experiments. This thesis builds such a representation in the form of a regime-aware ontological knowledge graph, supported by a numerical-encoding pipeline that retains both directions of mechanical change.

## 1.2 Research Question

This thesis examines whether an explicitly regime-aware representation of the polymer/clay literature, implemented as an ontological knowledge graph in the spirit of Buehler (2024), enables structure-property knowledge extraction that is not accessible to existing regime-blind analyses of the same data. The research questions are formulated as follows.

**RQ1.** Does promoting mechanical-property regimes to first-class graph entities — twenty-eight regimes organized as four uniform levels on each of seven property axes, rather than latent strata of a flat statistical model — alter the structure-property patterns that are surfaced by graph-based analysis of polymer/clay data, and if so, how?

**RQ2.** Can the negative-improvement portion of the data, which is systematically lost under the standard $\log_{10}$ transformation of property-improvement columns, be recovered by an arcsinh transformation, and does the recovered ductility-degradation signal change the conclusions that can be drawn about clay reinforcement?

**RQ3.** When the generative knowledge-graph framework of Buehler (2024), originally formulated for unstructured text, is adapted to a structured experimental dataset of polymer/clay nanocomposites, how does the resulting graph topology (degree distribution, scale-freeness, community structure) compare to the text-derived case, and what does that comparison say about the information density of structured versus unstructured materials data?

**RQ4.** Do transformer-based node embeddings of categorical concepts in the polymer/clay graph recover chemistry-meaningful structure — in particular, the polyamide family, dispersion-morphology variants, and the thermoset/thermoplastic/elastomer split — without any explicit chemical priors, and can such embeddings therefore serve as a domain-independent vehicle for knowledge extraction on tabular materials data?

## 1.3 Contributions

The main contributions of this thesis are as follows.

1. **A regime-aware representation of the polymer/clay literature with a uniform $4 \times 7$ structure.** Seven mechanical-property axes — three matrix-state axes (modulus, strength, strain-to-failure), three response axes (modulus, strength, and strain-to-failure improvement), and one loading axis (MMT weight fraction) — are each partitioned into four physically meaningful, literature-standard levels, yielding twenty-eight regime bins in total. Every bin is promoted to a first-class node of the ontological knowledge graph alongside the dataset's categorical concepts. Each of the 942 experiments is therefore explicitly co-located with both the chemistry it represents and the seven-dimensional mechanical regime it occupies, which makes within-regime structure–property analysis possible at the level of the full dataset rather than at the level of individual case studies. The uniform $4 \times 7$ structure was chosen deliberately so that no axis is over- or under-resolved and so that the regime grid can be compared, combined, and visualized symmetrically.

2. **A negative-compatible numerical-encoding pipeline.** A six-column arcsinh transformation is introduced alongside the existing $\log_{10}$ columns of the dataset. Because $\mathrm{arcsinh}$ is defined for all real numbers and behaves like $\log$ for large $|x|$ while remaining linear near zero, it preserves the negative-improvement data that $\log_{10}$ discards. For the elongation-improvement column the number of usable rows grew from 91 (under $\log_{10}$) to 440 (under $\mathrm{arcsinh}$), a 4.8-fold recovery, allowing the regime-aware analysis to incorporate ductility losses on equal footing with stiffness gains.

3. **A tabular-data adaptation of the Buehler (2024) generative knowledge-graph framework.** The Buehler pipeline — LLM-based triplet extraction, transformer-based node embedding, cosine-similarity-driven path traversal — was originally formulated for unstructured paper corpora. This thesis presents the first application of that framework to a structured, experiment-level polymer/clay dataset, replacing the stochastic LLM-extraction stage with deterministic concept extraction from cleaned categorical columns while retaining the downstream embedding, similarity, and traversal machinery. The substitution makes graph construction fully reproducible without sacrificing the semantic capabilities that the Buehler framework was designed to expose.

4. **A three-layer hybrid graph that fuses concepts, experiments, and property regimes.** The complete graph constructed in this work contains 245 categorical concept nodes, 942 experiment-level sample nodes, and 28 property-regime nodes, totalling 1,215 nodes and 13,394 edges. Concept-to-concept edges encode co-occurrence in a shared experiment; sample-to-concept edges anchor every individual measurement to the categorical concepts it instantiates; sample-to-regime edges anchor every measurement to the mechanical regimes it occupies. This three-layer construction makes every datum in the dataset addressable from any of the three perspectives.

5. **Empirical validation that text-only transformer embeddings recover polymer chemistry.** Sentence-transformer embeddings (`all-MiniLM-L6-v2`, 384-dimensional) of the node descriptions cluster the polyamide family (PA, PA6, PA7, PA8, PA12, PA66 and the PA6/PP blend) with pairwise cosine similarities in the range 0.83–0.97, group dispersion-morphology variants in a single sub-region, and resolve the thermoset/thermoplastic/elastomer split. Because the embedding model has no explicit chemical knowledge, this recovery is a domain-relevant analogue of the validation Buehler reports for biological materials, and supports the use of general-purpose embeddings as a knowledge-extraction tool on tabular materials data.

6. **A reproducible dataset-cleanup pipeline for polymer/clay literature.** The source dataset is normalized for case and whitespace inconsistencies across all categorical fields, semantically equivalent entries are merged (PA6 $\leftrightarrow$ Nylon 6; PA66 $\leftrightarrow$ Nylon66; Three-Point Bend $\leftrightarrow$ Flexural Test (3PB); multiple variants of "intercalated/exfoliated"), the `?` missing-data marker is converted to `NaN` so that downstream numerical operations treat absence correctly, and a single unit-error row is corrected (Epoxy matrix strength $0.12 \rightarrow 12$ MPa). Each cleanup stage is backed up so that all transformations remain auditable.

7. **Generative reasoning over the knowledge graph with experimentally testable design hypotheses.** Five sub-graphs are extracted from the complete graph to span thematically distinct regions of the polymer/clay design space, and an LLM is prompted to propose a composite design suggested by — but not yet realised in — each sub-graph. Five hypotheses (H1–H5) are produced; two of them (H2, a thermoset over-loading failure mechanism, and H5, a CNBR + unmodified-MMT cost-driven design) survive a skeptical cross-framing analysis as graph-derived novelties rather than restatements of prior domain knowledge. For H5 an explicit experimental falsification protocol is given — 11 compositions × 3 batches × $\geq$ 5 tensile replicates, with mean $\Delta E < +200\%$ at 15 wt% Na-MMT as the falsification criterion — bringing the generative-reasoning step (Buehler 2024 §2.3) to a testable engineering proposition.

8. **A comprehensive empirical validation of the embedding pipeline.** Following recommendations from the supervisor, the embedding methodology is validated along four orthogonal axes. First, every concept-node description is augmented with one-to-two sentences of polymer- and clay-nanocomposite domain context, doubling the intra/inter cosine cohesion gap from 0.086 to 0.173 and improving the silhouette score and the Adjusted Rand Index against ground-truth polymer-family labels. Second, the same enriched descriptions are re-encoded with five additional transformer models — `all-MiniLM-L6-v2` (general English baseline), `SciBERT`, `MatSciBERT`, `MaterialsBERT`, and the LBNL `MatBERT` of Walker et al. (2021) — and the resulting six embedding sets are compared head-to-head. Third, three quantitative cluster-quality scores (silhouette, Adjusted Rand Index, and Normalized Mutual Information) are computed against seven ground-truth polymer families, supplemented by a Spearman correlation between embedding distance and graph shortest-path distance, so that the comparison of models is by measurement rather than by visual inspection. Fourth, the embeddings are used as auxiliary features in a Gradient Boosting regressor predicting the arcsinh-transformed improvement of three mechanical properties: modulus ($\Delta E$, $n = 919$, $R^2$ from 0.309 to 0.346 with SciBERT/MatBERT), strength ($\Delta\sigma$, $n = 727$, $R^2$ from 0.336 to 0.365 with enriched MiniLM), and strain to failure ($\Delta\varepsilon$, $n = 440$, all embeddings degrade $R^2$ slightly). The modulus and strength tasks see relative $R^2$ gains of roughly 10–12 % over a categorical-only baseline, while the strain task — dominated by negative-ductility responses already captured by categorical encoding — shows that the embeddings are not a universal improvement, an honest result that delineates the conditions under which the methodology contributes useful information from the conditions under which it does not.

## 1.4 Structure of Thesis

This thesis consists of eight chapters. Chapter 1 presents the motivation, the regime-blindness problem, the research questions, and the contributions. Chapter 2 reviews relevant literature on polymer/clay nanocomposite mechanical-property modelling, the limited prior treatments of regime dependence, materials-informatics representations of structured experimental data, and the emerging family of generative and graph-based knowledge-extraction methods in materials science. Chapter 3 provides background on the polymer/clay system itself, the categorical and numerical fields of the source dataset, the definition of mechanical regimes used in this work, the Buehler generative knowledge-graph framework, and the basics of sentence-transformer node embeddings. Chapter 4 describes the methodology: the dataset-cleanup pipeline, the arcsinh transformation, the rules for deterministic concept extraction, the construction of the regime layer, the embedding pipeline, and the analytic procedures (community detection, hub identification, semantic neighbour retrieval, path-based reasoning) that operate on the resulting graph. Chapter 5 details the experimental setup, including the precise threshold choices for the regime layer, the configuration of the embedding model, and the software environment. Chapter 6 presents the results in three parts. The first part covers dataset-cleanup statistics, regime populations, graph-topology metrics (Buehler Table 1 analogue), community structure (full and concept-only), the centrality hierarchy, semantic-neighbour examples, complete-graph property-regime populations, and the validation of polymer-family recovery. The second part presents the empirical validation of the embedding pipeline along the four axes of the supervisor follow-up: description-enrichment effect, six-model comparison, three quantitative cluster-quality scores (silhouette, ARI, NMI), and the Spearman correlation between embedding distance and graph distance. The third part reports the predictive validation in which embeddings are used as auxiliary features to predict the arcsinh-transformed improvements of modulus, strength, and strain to failure. Chapter 7 discusses the implications of the findings, examines the regime-aware versus regime-blind comparison in detail, addresses validity threats, notes the limitations introduced by the deterministic-extraction choice, and presents a supplementary generative-reasoning study in which five polymer/clay design hypotheses (H1–H5) are produced by an LLM reasoning over selected sub-graphs of the complete graph, two of which (H2 and H5) survive a skeptical cross-framing and are accompanied by explicit falsification criteria — with H5 (CNBR + unmodified MMT) given a full experimental validation protocol. The chapter additionally documents the supervisor-recommended empirical strengthening of the embedding pipeline — description enrichment, six-model comparison, quantitative cluster-quality evaluation, and three-target predictive validation — as separate sections that complement and corroborate the generative-reasoning study. Chapter 8 summarizes the work, outlines its implications for materials-informatics practice, and identifies the Buehler-framework components that this thesis explicitly leaves to future work (LLM-driven graph reasoning, isomorphism analysis across material families, agentic data augmentation).
